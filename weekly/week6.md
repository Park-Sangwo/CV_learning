## 1. Mirror-based eyes & corneal imaging
Scallop Eyes - Uses concave parabolic mirrors instead of lenses to focus light onto receptors like a telescope.

Corneal Imaging - Human cornea with a tear film acts as an ellipsoidal reflective mirror.

We can find the corneal boundary(limbus) in a photo reveals its 3D orientation, enabling software to map the environment image and estimate the exact retinal image falling on the person's fovea.

## 2. Primitive eyes & eye evolution
Trilobite Eye - The oldest known fossilized visual system; a compound eye with thousands of tiny lenses made of transparent Calcite (CaCO3).

Limpet Eye - Lacks a lens but utilizes a curved image sensor to obtain spatial distribution and directional sensitivity of light.

Nautilus Eye - Utilizes a large pinhole structure to form images without any lens.

Eye Evolution Simulation - Computer simulation shows a flat light-sensitive tissue curves first for directionality, then develops a lens for brightness, taking approx. 400,000 generations.

## 3. Human visual system
Cornea provides initial bending power. 
Fluid-filled gelatinous lens changes shape via ciliary muscles to adjust focal length. 
Curved retina(rods and cones) acts as a sensor where the fovea provides maximum resolution.

**Iris Control System** - Functions like a camera diaphragm to regulate pupil size.
An edge-lit narrow beam experiment triggers iris oscillation, characterizing its neural control loop.

**Accommodation** - Distant vision relaxes the lens; near vision squishes the lens to increase bending power. 
Accommodation power decreases with age as the fluid lens hardens.

**Myopia (Near-Sightedness)** - Occurs when the lens has excessive bending power, causing images of distant objects to form in front of the retina. 
Corrected using a diverging (concave) lens.
