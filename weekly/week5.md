## 1. Lens related issues & compound lenses
Compound lenses are used because it is difficult to maintain uniform image quality across the entire image (periphery) with a single lens alone.

Even if a lens is perfectly manufactured, it turns out that it will still produce some aberrations, or undesirable effects.

**vignetting** - phenomenon where the edges of the image become dark as light from the periphery, deviating from the optical axis, is blocked by the compound lens structure.

**chromatic aberration** - Because the refractive index of the lens varies depending on the wavelength (color) of light, blue light bends significantly and red light bends less. This causes some shifts in color in the image.

**geometric aberrations or distortions** 

In the case of radial distortion, as we move away from the center of the image, points tend to get pushed out more and more. 
As a result, there is an apparent bulging of the image, also known as barrel distortion.

In the case of tangential distortion, there is a slight twisting of the image. As we go farther away from the center of the image the twisting increases.

## 2. wide angle cameras and catadioptric systems
Capturing an unusually large field of view (e.g., hemispherical or 360-degree) requires specialized compression designs.

**Fisheye Lens** - Uses a series of meniscus lenses to severely bend light rays and compress a hemisphere onto a small image sensor.

**Catadioptric System** - A system combining mirrors (catoptrics) and lenses (dioptrics)
Expand the field of view well beyond a hemisphere while maintaining a single viewpoint constraint via software mapping.

Planar Mirrors - Used for optical folding to change camera position/orientation without changing the field of view size.

**hyperbolic mirror** - has two foci. All the rays of light that come in the direction of the first focus, Focus 1, get reflected by the hyperbolic mirror towards the second focus, Focus 2.

**parabolic mirror** - has a single focus. It takes all the rays of light that come in the direction of its focus and reflects these rays to be parallel to one another.
